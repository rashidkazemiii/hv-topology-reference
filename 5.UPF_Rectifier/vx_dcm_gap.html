What Vx does when all four bridge diodes are off

In DCM, the input current i1 reaches zero and both diode pairs block. A natural guess is that the bridge voltage Vx just keeps its last value, +2VC. This page shows step by step, with the paper's own equations, why it cannot: the coupled inductor forces Vx to a new level, Vx = vin − nk·V2. In a real circuit, the diode capacitance makes Vx ring into that level rather than jump. Every number below updates with the sliders.

1The two equations everything comes from

Winding L1 carries the input current i1, and winding L2 carries i2. Their voltages are coupled through the mutual inductance M (paper eqs. 1–2):

V1=L1di1dt+Mdi2dtV2=Mdi1dt+L2di2dt
Solved for the slopes (paper eq. 3), with det = L1·L2 − M²:
di1dt=L2V1−MV2detdi2dt=L1V2−MV1det
and the two loop equations that connect them to the circuit:
V1=vin−VxV2=−VC (S1 on) or+VC (S2 on)

2Who sets Vx, interval by interval

V2 is always set by the half bridge. Vx is set by whichever diode pair conducts. When a pair conducts it connects the bridge input to the bus, and Vx is clamped to ±2VC. When no pair conducts, nothing clamps Vx, and it is set by the only other thing connected to it: winding L1.

3Derivation: Vx in the gap

In the gap, i1 = 0 and stays 0 until a diode pair turns on again. A current that stays constant has zero slope, so di1/dt = 0. Put that into the first slope equation:

di1dt=0⇒L2V1=MV2⇒V1=ML2V2=nk·V2
L1 still has a voltage across it even with no current: the voltage induced by the changing i2. The second winding equation, with di1/dt = 0, gives that change:
V2=L2di2dt⇒di2dt=V2L2andV1=Mdi2dt=nk·V2(same result)
Finally, the loop equation turns V1 into Vx:
Vx=vin−V1=vin−nk·V2(positive half cycle, S2 on:Vx=vin−nk·VC)

Nothing in this expression changes during the gap: vin is constant over one switching period, and VC and V2 are fixed. So Vx is constant through the gap, but at this new level, not at +2VC.

4Check by contradiction: what if Vx stayed at +2VC?

Vx=+2VC⇒V1=vin−2VC⇒di1dt=L2(vin−2VC)−MVCdet=vin−(nk+2)VCLσ
This slope is negative (in DCM, vin < (nk+2)·VC, always true because 2VC > Vm). Starting from i1 = 0, a negative slope means i1 must go negative at once. That needs D2 and D3 to conduct, which needs Vx = −2VC, but we assumed +2VC. The assumption contradicts itself, so Vx cannot stay at +2VC.

Here Lσ = det/L2 = L1·(1 − k²) is the leakage inductance seen from the input winding. It shows up because L2·V1 − M·V2 = L2·(V1 − nk·V2), so di1/dt = (V1 − nk·V2)/Lσ. In words: i1 only changes when V1 differs from nk·V2. That is the cleanest way to see the whole story: holding i1 = 0 needs V1 = nk·V2 exactly.

5Why both diode pairs stay off

−2VC<vin−nk·VC<+2VC⟺(nk−2)VC<vin<(nk+2)VC
The left inequality is exactly the DCM condition, the opposite of the paper's IRCM condition (18). The right one always holds. So whenever the converter is in DCM, the level Vx = vin − nk·VC automatically lies between the two clamps, and the gap is self-consistent.

6The fingerprint in the paper's Fig. 3a: the kink in i2

Compare the slope of i2 just before the gap (interval 3, D1·D4 on, S2 on) and inside the gap:

(di2dt)before=L1VC−M(vin−2VC)det(di2dt)gap=VCL2 before − gap=Mdet·[(nk+2)VC−vin]>0
(Uses L1/det − 1/L2 = M²/(L2·det).) The difference is positive, so i2 rises more slowly after the gap starts: a kink at t3. That is exactly what the paper's Fig. 3a shows. If Vx stayed at +2VC, V1 and V2 would not change at t3, so neither could the slope of i2, and there would be no kink.

The same bracket also gives the size of the Vx step at t3: ΔVx = (vin − nk·VC) − 2VC = −[(nk+2)·VC − vin]. One quantity sets both the voltage jump and the slope kink.

7A real circuit: Vx rings into the new level

The bridge input node always has some capacitance Cx: diode junction capacitance plus winding and layout capacitance. With it, i1 is no longer exactly zero in the gap. It charges Cx. Combine Cx·dVx/dt = i1 with the leakage form of the first equation:

Lσdi1dt=vin−Vx−nk·V2=Ve−Vx⇒LσCxd2Vxdt2=Ve−Vx
A simple LC oscillator around Ve = vin − nk·V2. Starting from Vx = +2VC with i1 = 0, and with damping ratio ζ:
Vx(t)≈Ve+(2VC−Ve)·e−ζω0tcos(ω0t),ω0=1LσCx,Lσ=L1(1−k2)

So on a scope, Vx neither holds at +2VC nor jumps cleanly: it starts at +2VC and rings around Ve until the ringing dies out. The undamped swing reaches 2Ve − 2VC. If that goes below −2VC (whenever Ve < 0), D2·D3 clip the first swing and pass a short pulse of reverse current. As Cx → 0 the ringing gets infinitely fast and the ideal step of section 3 is the limit.

8Summary

During the gap there is no diode to clamp Vx, so it is set by L1. Holding i1 = 0 requires L1's voltage to equal the voltage induced from L2: V1 = nk·V2. Therefore Vx = vin − nk·V2, constant during the gap but different from the ±2VC of the previous interval. The paper's Fig. 3a shows the consequence as a kink in i2. On real hardware the node capacitance turns the step into a decaying ring around the same level.
