5.UPF_Rectifier · single-stage converter · magnetic design

One core for the coupled inductor, the LLC transformer and Lr

The single-stage converter (Dornbirn rectifier + half-bridge LLC on S1–S2) has three magnetic parts: the coupled inductor L1–L2, the LLC transformer and the resonant inductor Lr. This page first reduces the coupled inductor to what its job really needs. Then it puts it on the same E-core as the transformer, using two flux modes that do not couple, and solves for turns, air gaps, shunts, flux density and losses. The electrical values come from the single-stage design: 230 Vac, 48 V / 60 W, bus 380 V at full load, fs = 100 kHz, nk = 4.

0The circuit, and where each winding sits

1What the coupled inductor really is

A coupled inductor (L1, L2, M) is equivalent to an ideal transformer N1:N2 with a magnetizing inductance on one side and a leakage inductance on the other. Put all leakage on the input side W1 and choose the turns ratio N1/N2 = nk:

L1 = nk²·L2 + Lσ1 M = nk·L2 nk = M/L2 = N1/N2 Lσ1 = L1 − M²/L2 = L1·(1 − k²) = det/L2 (det = L1·L2 − M²)

Now rewrite every rectifier formula of the ECCE paper with these two parameters. Γ11 = L2/det = 1/Lσ1 and Γ12 = −M/det = −nk/Lσ1, so:

input slope: di1/dt = (V1 − nk·V2) / Lσ1 input resistance: R_in = 8·fs·√(L1·L2)·(1 − k²)/k = 8·fs·det/M = 8·fs·Lσ1 / nk input current: I_in = nk·T·v_in / (8·Lσ1) IRCM condition: v_in < (nk − 2)·V_C (only nk) magnetizing side: d(i2 + nk·i1)/dt = V2 / L2

The result: the whole input side depends only on Lσ1 and nk. L2 does not appear. L2 only sets how much triangular current i2 circulates in the half bridge (ZVS current and RMS loss). So for magnetic design there are three separate jobs: a leakage inductance Lσ1 that carries the line current, a turns ratio nk, and a magnetizing inductance L2 that you can choose freely.

Cross-check with the PCIM paper. Its topology uses a separate buffer inductor Lf and two coupled windings LC2, LC3 of ratio nC each, one in each line. That is exactly this equivalent circuit, with Lf = Lσ1 and total ratio nk = 2·nC. Substituting into Iin = nk·T·vin/(8·Lσ1) gives I = n_C·T·V_in/(4·L_f), which is the PCIM paper's eq. 14 exactly. The two papers describe the same mechanism.

2The flux each part must carry

A winding's flux linkage λ = N·Φ sets the turns and core area. All three parts have closed forms:

coupled inductor, magnetizing path (W1 + W2): λo(t) = L2·(i2 + nk·i1) = nk·L2·i_in(t) + triangle of peak V_C/(4·fs) λo,pk = nk·L2·I_in,pk + V_C/(4·fs) I_in,pk = V_m/R_in (50 Hz part + switching part) input leakage Lσ1 (line current): λσ1,pk = Lσ1·i1,pk LLC transformer, magnetizing (at resonance, full load): λc,pk = N·V_o/(4·fs) i_m,pk = N·V_o/(4·fs·Lm)

The 50 Hz term in λo exists because the local average of i2 is zero, while i1 carries the line current. So the coupled inductor carries a line-frequency flux bias, like a PFC choke. The transformer flux has no DC or 50 Hz part. Lowering L2 shrinks the 50 Hz term but raises the i2 ripple ΔI2 = V_C/(4·fs·L2), so there is an optimum.

3One E-core, two flux modes that do not see each other

Why the two modes are decoupled. By symmetry (equal outer legs and gaps):

transformer flux seen by an anti-series outer pair: (N/2)·dΦc/2/dt − (N/2)·dΦc/2/dt = 0 circulating flux through the center leg: Φo,center = 0 (equal and opposite MMF paths) → mutual inductance between the coupled inductor and the transformer = 0

The two parts share the outer legs, so their fluxes add in one outer leg and subtract in the other. That is the one constraint the design must respect:

center leg: Bc = Φc / Ac outer legs: Bo = (Φc/2 ± Φo) / Ao worst leg: Φc,pk/2 + Φo,pk ≤ Bmax·Ao

4Design equations

1. transformer turns: Np ≥ λc,pk / (Bc,target·Ac), Ns = Np / N (integer pair closest to N) 2. coupled turns: N2 ≥ λo,eff / (Bmax·Ao − Φc,pk/2), N1 = nk·N2 λo,eff = λo,pk (option A: Lσ1 as a separate choke) λo,eff = λo,pk + Lσ1·i1,pk/nk (option B: Lσ1 by shunts on the same core) 3. outer gaps (L2): R_o = N2² / (2·L2), g_o = μ0·Ao·(R_o − R_fe,o) 4. center gap (Lm): Lm = Np² / (R_c + R_o/2), g_c = μ0·Ac·(Np²/Lm − R_o/2 − R_fe,c) (needs g_c ≥ 0) 5. Lr shunt: Lr = Np² / R_sh, g_r = μ0·A_sh·Np² / Lr 6. Lσ1 shunts (B only): Lσ1 = N1² / (2·R_s1), g_s1 = μ0·Ao·N1² / (2·Lσ1) 7. core loss: Pv = 70 kW/m³ · (f/100 kHz)^1.3 · (B_ac/0.1 T)^2.5 (ferrite, ~100 °C, approx.) 8. copper: Aw,needed = Σ N·I_rms / (J·k_fill), P_cu = ρ·J·MLT·Σ N·I_rms

Rfe is the small ferrite part of each path (μr ≈ 2000). Fringing at the gaps is neglected, which makes real gaps slightly larger than computed. Option A keeps Lσ1 as a separate small choke, exactly like the PCIM prototype's Lf. Option B also puts Lσ1 on the core through two symmetric shunts. Their leakage flux enters the center leg with opposite signs from the two halves, so it cancels there too.

5Solve it

6What is gained, and what still has to be proven

  • Part count: coupled inductor + transformer + Lr (three cores) become one core with two outer gaps, one center gap and one or three shunts. Option A keeps one small choke for Lσ1. Its size follows from the area product AP = Lσ1·i1,pk·i1,rms/(Bmax·J·k_fill), shown in the table.
  • Shared iron: in a separate transformer the outer legs only return Φc/2. Here the same legs also carry the coupled-inductor flux, so iron that was half used now does two jobs.
  • Novelty and paper potential: the reduction in section 1 (Rin = 8·fs·Lσ1/nk, with L2 free) and the decoupled two-mode core give a design method specific to IRCM rectifiers. The L2 optimum and the flux-sharing limit are design results you can publish together with a prototype.
  • Limits of this model: reluctances are lumped, fringing is ignored, the core-loss law is approximate, the leakage shunts are idealized and the ETD window and MLT values are approximate. The next steps are a 3-D FEM check of decoupling, shunt reluctances and the flux split, then a prototype measurement of L1, L2, M, Lm and Lr.
