A rectifier with inherent unity power factor

A diode bridge D1–D4 feeds two stacked bulk capacitors, Cb1 and Cb2. A half bridge S1–S2 runs at a fixed 50% duty. The two sides are tied together only by a coupled inductor: winding L1 sits in the AC line, and winding L2 sits between the capacitor midpoint and the half-bridge node. There is no current loop and no current sensing. Each switching period, the input current i1 swings positive and then briefly negative. The paper calls this the inherent reverse current mode (IRCM). That reverse part exactly cancels the error of the forward part, so the local average of i1 is Vin/Rin: the converter looks like a resistor to the grid.

The three design conditions

No CCM

|vin|/2 ≤ VC

From flux balance, tα − tβ = T·vin/(4VC). Interval 3 must fit in half a period, so the bulk stack 2VC only has to sit just above the mains peak (≈ 325 V for 230 Vrms).

IRCM, so unity PF

|vin|/(nk − 2) ≤ VC

This keeps the reverse current Iβ alive. Where it fails the converter drops into DCM and the input current is only the forward part. With nk = 4 it reduces to the no-CCM condition, so VC ≥ Vm/2 gives UPF over the whole cycle.

No zero-crossing distortion

nk > 2

At vin = 0 the reverse slope is VC(2L2 − M)/det, which is negative only if M > 2L2. Then the bridge diodes keep conducting through the zero crossing, and the usual PFC dead zone around the zero crossing disappears.

Model settings: Vin = 230 Vrms (Vm = 325 V), fL = 50 Hz, fs = 100 kHz, P = 70 W as in the paper's Figs. 4–6, and coupling k = 0.9 (the paper leaves k free). L1, L2 and M follow from Rin = Vm²/2P and the chosen nk. i2 is shifted so its local average is zero, which is the paper's charge-balance condition for Cb1 and Cb2. The gate signals include the fixed 2% dead time after t0 and after t2. V2 still steps exactly at t0 and t2, as the paper assumes: i2 is large at both edges, so it swings the switch node through Coss in a few tens of nanoseconds, and the body diode of the incoming switch carries i2 for the rest of the dead time (shown in the schematic). UPF Rectifier Transitions extends this model: it gives the switch-node swing, the diode commutation through Cj and the ringing in the DCM gap intervals of their own.
