2.LLC · Full-bridge LLC primary

How the active full bridge drives an LLC tank

S1–S4 form the active full bridge. The switches run in diagonal pairs, S1·S4 then S2·S3, each at 50% duty with a short dead time between them. That turns Vin into a ±Vin square wave vab. The series tank Cr–Lr filters it into a near-sinusoidal current ir, and the diode bridge D5–D8 rectifies it into Vo. The switching frequency fs sets the gain. The magnetizing current im in Lm is what gives every switch zero-voltage turn-on.

Three operating regions

The bridge itself always does the same thing. What changes with fs is when the tank current crosses zero relative to the switching edges, and that decides whether the switches keep ZVS and whether the rectifier diodes turn off softly.

Below resonance, fs < fr

The resonant half-wave finishes before the switches change state. The rectifier current falls to zero on its own (ZCS), then only im flows until the dead time. Gain rises above 1. Too far down, or too much load, and the tank turns capacitive and ZVS is lost.

At resonance, fs = fr

Cr and Lr cancel, so gain is 1 regardless of load. ir is a sine that meets im right at the switching edge. This is the most efficient point, and an LLC-DCX runs fixed here.

Above resonance, fs > fr

The switches change state before the half-wave ends, so rectifier current is cut off while still flowing, and the diodes see reverse recovery. Primary ZVS is easy here. Gain falls below 1.

Waveforms come from a time-domain simulation of the tank with an ideal diode rectifier and a stiff output: Vin = 1, Lr = Cr = 1, secondary values referred to the primary, dead time 3.5% of the period. The Coss swing is modelled as a linear ramp of vab during the first part of the dead time.
