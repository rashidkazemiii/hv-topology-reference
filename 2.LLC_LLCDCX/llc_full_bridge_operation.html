2.LLC · Full-bridge LLC with synchronous rectifier

Two active full bridges: the LLC inverter and its synchronous rectifier

S1–S4 form the active full bridge. The switches run in diagonal pairs, S1·S4 then S2·S3, each at 50% duty with a short dead time between them. That turns Vin into a ±Vin square wave vab. The series tank Cr–Lr filters it into a near-sinusoidal current ir, and the secondary active bridge S5–S8 rectifies it into Vo. That secondary bridge is a synchronous rectifier (SR): each MOSFET is turned on while its body diode would conduct, so the current flows through the channel with an I·Rds(on) drop instead of a ~0.7 V diode drop. The switching frequency fs sets the gain. The magnetizing current im in Lm is what gives every switch zero-voltage turn-on.

Three operating regions

The bridge itself always does the same thing. What changes with fs is when the tank current crosses zero relative to the switching edges, and that decides whether the switches keep ZVS and whether the SR MOSFETs turn off at zero current.

Below resonance, fs < fr

The resonant half-wave finishes before the switches change state. The secondary current falls to zero on its own, so the SR MOSFETs turn off at zero current (ZCS), then only im flows until the dead time. Gain rises above 1. Too far down, or too much load, and the tank turns capacitive and ZVS is lost.

At resonance, fs = fr

Cr and Lr cancel, so gain is 1 regardless of load. ir is a sine that meets im right at the switching edge. This is the most efficient point, and an LLC-DCX runs fixed here.

Above resonance, fs > fr

The switches change state before the half-wave ends, so the SR MOSFETs must turn off while current still flows. That current drops into their body diodes for the dead time, which then see reverse recovery. Primary ZVS is easy here. Gain falls below 1.

Waveforms come from a time-domain simulation of the tank with an ideal synchronous rectifier and a stiff output. The SR controller turns each MOSFET on 2% of a period after its body diode starts conducting, holds it off during the primary dead time, and turns it off when the current reaches zero: Vin = 1, Lr = Cr = 1, secondary values referred to the primary, dead time 3.5% of the period. The Coss swing is modelled as a linear ramp of vab during the first part of the dead time.
