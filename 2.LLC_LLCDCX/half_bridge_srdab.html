2.LLC_LLCDCX · half bridge on both sides · TI SDAA372

Half-bridge resonant converter, primary and secondary, step by step

Two half bridges, one on each side of a transformer, with Lr and Cr in between. Every switch runs at 50 % duty with a short dead time. The secondary is delayed by a phase shift θ, and θ decides how much power flows. Press play and follow the gate signals.

step 1

1The circuit and its waveforms, side by side

How a half bridge makes a square wave. The two capacitors split the input voltage, so their midpoint sits at half of it. When the top switch is on, the switch node is +V/2 above the midpoint. When the bottom switch is on, it is −V/2. The primary makes ±Vbus/2 at node A, and the secondary makes ±VBat/2 at node B. Lr and Cr sit between the two square waves, and the current depends on the difference between them.

  1. Primary dead time. Q2 has turned off and Q1 is not on yet. The current is still negative, so it flows into node A and charges it up from −12 V to +12 V. Once node A reaches +12 V, Q1's body diode starts conducting.
  2. Q1 turns on at zero voltage. Its voltage is already zero (the diode was conducting), so there is no switching loss. The current is still negative and flows backwards through Q1. The tank sees the largest voltage of the period, vA − vB/n = +12 + 12 V, so the current rises fast.
  3. Current crosses zero, while Q4 is still on. Now Q1 conducts normally, but the secondary has not switched yet. Current flows against vB, so a little power goes back from the battery. This short "backflow" interval is the price of the phase shift.
  4. Secondary dead time. Q4 turns off. The now-positive current charges node B up to +VBat/2, and Q3's body diode takes over.
  5. Q3 turns on at zero voltage. Power flows. Both bridges are now positive. The tank sees only vA − vB/n, which is small, so the current changes slowly while it delivers energy to the battery.
  6. Second half. Q1 turns off, and the same six steps repeat mirrored: Q2 instead of Q1, Q4 instead of Q3, all signs reversed.

3Zoom: what happens inside the dead time

The rule for ZVS, in one sentence: when a switch turns off, the current must still be flowing in the direction that swings the node toward the other rail, and it must be large enough to finish the swing within the dead time.

swing time ≈ 2·C_oss · V / |i| must be < dead time primary (Q1): i_res < 0 when Q2 turns off secondary (Q3): i_res > 0 when Q4 turns off

Try it. Lower θ to about 10°: the current at the switching edges gets small, the swing becomes slow, and the switch turns on before the node arrives (red). Raise VBat to 90 V: the primary loses ZVS first. Lower it to 30 V: the secondary loses it first. A dead time that is too long is not free either. After the swing, the body diode conducts for the rest of the dead time, and that costs conduction loss.

4Why can't θ be zero?

It can: move the θ slider to 0° and look at the result. Two things go wrong.

  • No power. The power is P = 2M·V_bus²·sin θ/(π²·X). At θ = 0 the two square waves are in phase, so sin θ = 0 and nothing is transferred. If VBat = n·Vbus (M = 1), the tank sees no voltage at all, and the current almost disappears.
  • No ZVS. The dead-time swing is driven by the current at the switching edge. With almost no current, the node cannot swing, and all four switches turn on with voltage across them: hard switching (red circles). With M ≠ 1 some current flows, but it only circulates (reactive) and it helps just one side.

So θ is the power knob. Increasing θ from 0 toward 90° increases the power and the ZVS current. A negative θ reverses the power: the secondary leads, and energy flows from the battery to the bus. That is the reverse mode in the app note.

5How to choose θ (TI app note SDAA372)

The app note's goal: pick θ from the voltage ratio M = V_Bat/(n·V_bus) so that all four switches keep ZVS, and set the power with the switching frequency. With the first-harmonic approximation, both sides have ZVS when

θ > θ_boundary = arccos[ min(M, 1/M) ] (correct in the app note)

What I found when checking the app note:

  • Eq. 6 is wrong. It forgets that an inductive tank makes the current lag by 90°, the "j" in jX. The correct current is i ∝ M·sinθ·sin ωt − (1 − M·cosθ)·cos ωt. The later equations 11 and 13 already use this correct form.
  • Eq. 14–15 are curve fits, not solutions. Eq. 13 has no answer below 90°.
  • Eq. 15 drops below the ZVS boundary for M > 1.6. There the primary switches hard. The cause is the extra factor (1 − 0.25·|M − 1|).

Simple fix: remove that factor.

θ = arccos[ 0.8 · min(M, 1/M) ]

6Numbers used

The simulation is a time-domain model of the real circuit. It uses ideal switches with body diodes, a constant Coss on each switch, the dead times, and a 20 mΩ tank resistance. It runs until the waveforms repeat. The app note's own PLECS values are not published, so these numbers are an example at a similar power level. The transformer magnetizing inductance is left out to keep it simple.
